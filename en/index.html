How a program is born

1

The president proposes, Congress approves

Many programs become law. The government sends the bill or provisional measure, deputies and senators vote and can change the text, and the president signs it. Emergency Aid came out of Congress at triple the proposed amount.

2

One program leads to another

Bolsa Família merged FHC's programs. Fundeb replaced Fundef. Luz para Todos expanded Luz no Campo. Creating and expanding are different things, and both count.

3

Renaming is not creating

Bolsonaro swapped Bolsa Família for Auxílio Brasil and Minha Casa Minha Vida for Casa Verde e Amarela. Lula brought back the old names in 2023. The goals continued, but rules, benefits and funding also changed. They were not just label swaps.

4

Some come from the Constitution

SUS, the BPC and the one-minimum-wage rural pension are in the 1988 Constitution, written by an elected Assembly. No government can claim them alone. Later governments regulated, paid for and expanded them.

Verdict

No government built today's network of programs alone. It was assembled in layers, and each layer has an owner.

  • 1988 Constitution: SUS, BPC and the one-minimum-wage rural pension.
  • FHC: Fundef, FIES, Pronaf, generic drugs and the first income programs, such as Bolsa Escola.
  • Lula: Bolsa Família, ProUni, Minha Casa Minha Vida, Farmácia Popular, SAMU, Luz para Todos, MEI and Fundeb. In the third term, Pé-de-Meia, Desenrola, Gás do Povo and the income tax exemption up to R$5,000.
  • Dilma: Mais Médicos, Pronatec, quotas at federal universities and the minimum wage appreciation law.
  • Bolsonaro: Emergency Aid (with the amount set by Congress), the new Auxílio Gás and Pix going live. He also replaced earlier programs, with changes of name and rules, and gave no real increase to the minimum wage.

Who created it matters. Who kept, expanded or cut it does too.
